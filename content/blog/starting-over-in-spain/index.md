This might be one of the hardest posts to write thus far. My previous one was very optimistic, full of hope. I felt I had given good battle and it was time to rest and enjoy. But as it so often happens, we mortals can't count on the fates to loom the threads of destiny the way we want.

A few months ago, my husband and I decided to move to Spain and start over. The reason is, as it most always is in cases like this, not simple. You can read about it in my previous post if you're interested, but the core of what we set out to seek is community, being closer to our family, friends and culture. This decision involved trading stability for a chance at a new beginning.

Long story short, we are both now unemployed. In this post I will talk about what happened in between now and then, and how we got to this point. 

> Disclaimer: literature rant, feel free to skip this paragraph.
>
> I've always leaned on literature to help create meaning of the world and my experiences.
>
> This time, it was Ursula Le Guin's Lavinium that acompannied me. It's a retelling of Virgil's myth through Lavinia, wife to Aeneas. I felt the connection to her, to Lavinia, and to humanity, as I sat outside in the evenings, reading, watching the same stars as Lavinia. As I struggled with the challenges of starting over, of being in between homes, away from my support system, eager to start building our new future. The nights I doubted myself, if my effort was enough, pushing myself.

I could try to write a cohesive, nicely-packed, narrative with a clear beginning, middle and end. The truth is that life is messy, and I won't try to pretend otherwise. Maybe if I had it all figured out already it'd be easier to write this in such a fashion, but I'm really writing as I process everything that has happened in the last couple of months.

Although it's not our first time moving countries, the process wasn't easy at all. First the many sleepless nights over maybe 2 years pondering over this decision. Is this the right choice of us? If so, how to execute it? There was a lot to plan.

## Interview Prep and Taking Up Rust

Then came the process of interview prep for us, which started slowly at first, by watching cppcon videos mainly, rekindling my motivation. Slowly, as I built momentum, I started reading books on operating systems, modern c++ best practices, concurrency... As things got more serious, I started putting everything into anki flashcards, griding leetcode and (surprise!) taking up Rust. My motivation for learning Rust was first of all curiosity, and second of all, it might sound contradictory, but the more I studied C++, the more I realized just how much I liked the langauge, and more and more my motivation grew to understand it at a deeper level. What does this have to do with Rust? Well, I felt that in order to truly understand a language,  I ought to have something to compare it to. Rust gave me exactly that. By reading about how Rust implements the same concepts I had been studying with C++, the underlying mechanics and solutions to problems faced by those designing the languages became more apparent.

You can imagine how overjoyed I was to receive a job offer for a C++ & Rust position, our plan was finally beginning to materialize, and I was thrilled at the learning opportunity.

## Moving Out

Moving out was full of so many emotions. Munich and Germany will forever hold a special part in my heart, part of my story. Every other time I had left a place felt like it was rushed and I feel as if I didn't really get the chance to properly say goodbye. Moving from Argentina to Russia:  we had been planning it for almost a year, but, long story short, when the final confirmation that we could go (invitation letter from the university), we had only a week to pack, make final arrangements and say goodbye. Until that moment, it wasn't certainty that we'd go, and didn't, therefore, felt truly real. I have a clear memory of cracking open a suitcase and going through my room and just throwing stuff in. Only a week to mentally prepare for the biggest change in my life so far. Next time: leaving Moscow and university as the war broke out. It all happened so fast it's all quite hazy now. Decisions had to be made quick, no time to process it, we had to focus on undoing and redoing our life. Why am I reminiscing about all of this? Well, because this time it felt cathartic to actually have time to plan and to properly say goodbye to what had been our home for 4 years. I am also glad the company let me take one last trip to Stuttgart to say goodbye to my colleagues. All of this was very important for me. It gave me closure.

## Arriving in Spain

 I moved to Spain to start my new job end of May. Santiago, my husband, had to continue working through his notice period until July. Pampu, our cat, stayed with him. To say it wasn't easy for either of us would be an understatement. It's just one of many challenges life throws at you that really put your relationship and resilience to the test.
 
I am forever grateful to my parents for providing me a stable place for me to live as we once again put our life together. As much as I was glad to see them and spend time with them, though, I would be lying if I didn't admit I struggled with starting a new remote job without my own space, away from my support system and my routine, in a different country. // Not to mention a health-related family situation, which I'll spare for the sake of their privacy, but which added to my emotional toll.

## The Struggles of a New Role Without Yet My Own Space

Anyway, I wanted to do well, to make a good first impression. I was tired from everything related to moving, but I was full of excitement for the future, both in terms of my career and my personal life. This was everything we had been planning for years. I was hungry to learn, ready to take the leap.

It wasn't easy. I wished I could have had my own space for working, though I knew that'd come in time. My parents helped me pick up an used desk and chair, which I bought on wallapop for my temporary setup, which would become my little home for the next month and a half.

I relied on the skills that had never failed me: I optimized my time as much as I could. I protected my routine at all cost, getting up early everyday to run because I knew that was the best way to keep my mind sharp throughout the day. I needed my mind sharp, to understand the new codebase and processes. Started reading about windows system programming (as my main experience so far had involved mostly working with linux). Created lots of anki cards to acquire the new concepts as quickly as possible. Breakfast was spent reviewing cards, while I made sure to stick to decaf and enough protein to fuel my morning. 

As time went by, I could feel myself getting more and more confortable in my new role. My teammates and manager were wonderful and very helpful as they taught me the ropes. I was overjoyed with each day's progress, both with what I was learning, but mostly realizing that I could make it and that the role was what I wanted. I saw myself growing, eager to take on more responsibility.

Meanwhile, our appartment-hunting efforts paid off, which meant that I could move in to what would become our new place. Santiago still had to finish his contract in Germany.

Moving is never easy, as I've already mentioned, but planning the logistics, each of us dealing with a separate set of problems, he on Germany, I on Spain, it was truly nervewreaking. Two countries notorious for their bourocracy, no need to explain more. We are used to handling these things together, so being apart was by far the biggest challenge for us. 

## Reminiscing on My Career Path and What Led Me Here

I remember in one of my first 1:1s with my manager at my first job, I told him I felt my weak point was concurrency. I am so glad I did because some time after, the company hired Reiner Grimm (rest his soul) to give a seminar on concurrency, to which my manager signed up me as one of the 15 participants. This piked my interest in the subject. Not only in concurrency, but in the role of the "educator". I was intrigued by what it was like, and wished that maybe someday I'd get to do something similar. It's crazy to think how much influence something seemingly not so important can influence the course of one's career and life. I don't consider myself an expert, but I do find so much joy in sharing what I'm learning through writing.

Another mentor I remember I asked for a recommendation of a book for learning linux (such an abstract question, I know), and he recommended me The Linux Programming Interface. It's not a book you sit down and read front to back, but I kept several advancing bookmarks. It helped me understand at a more fundamental level what systems programming meant. My previous programming experience had more to do with competitive programming and physics simulation, and I had disregarded some other aspects of software development, like operating systems and concurrency. Two topics that I would get more and more engulfed in throughout my career, though the farther I go, the more I realize how much path still lays ahead.

Now, I'm not sure what the future holds, but I'm leaning into Rust, with a heavy interest in the concurrency and low-level optimization side of things. I am forever grateful for the people that helped to shape my path.

## Cut Back to The Present

Cut to last Saturday, I'm playing magic the gathering with friends. The pieces start falling into place. Everything makes sense right in that moment. Life is simple. This is what I came such a long way for.

Becoming suddenly unemployed puts years of personal-work and your confidence to the test. It makes you question yourself, your choices, and whether you can make it again. It's nothing new though. It's at the core of what unites us all, through the centuries, part of the human experience. Isn't a company restructuring the modern day equivalent of a hailstorm, which our ancestors would pray the gods to spare them from? Our stories are not so unique after all. This thought brings me solace in that we are never truly alone. There's nothing to do but sow next cycle's crops.

I am grateful for the opportunity, for getting to be where I am now, which is where we had wanted to be all along.

I have never been alone throughout this process. On the battlefront has always been Santi, alongside me, getting through the hardest challenges together, words alone are not enough to describe my appreciation. And my family and friends, whose support I value immensely.

I might not have everything figured out yet. Might need to do it all over again, but I'm finding the meaning that lies in the process. Can Sisyphus wake up everyday knowing the boulder must be pushed up the mountain again and still manage to face it with a smile?
